    mosaicQ · Governance

    Building IT governance from zero in a remote software consultancy

    Problem
    Demands arrived by email, chat, meetings and videos. Sprints were created once and never closed, and nobody knew for sure what had been delivered.
    Approach
    Gradual change in live operations: pilot the method on one project, measure, then replicate. Four phases, each with a plan, a journal and a formal close.
    Outcome
    One entry channel triaged in or out of contract scope, weekly sprints with their own version, and every delivery communicated formally.

    How I worked

    • Pilot, then replicate
    • One door for demand
    • Measure before changing
    1. MayOperational base

      Demand funnel, ticket standard, sprint cadence.

    2. JunFirst indicators

      Contract compliance, Velocity and Cycle Time.

    3. Jul–SepIndicator panel

      Contracts, SLA, workload, planning.

    4. NowDecisions

      Data-driven meetings, post-mortems, risk.

    mosaicQ · Jira

    Two boards, one project: sprints for development, Kanban for support

    Problem
    Planned development and incoming support compete for the same people. Mixed on one board, neither the sprint nor the support queue can be read.
    Approach
    Development runs in sprints with story points and due dates; support runs as a continuous Kanban flow. Both share the same stages, review waits in its own column, and every ticket carries a contract label and a type label.
    Outcome
    The sprint shows what was committed, support shows what is waiting, and hours from both are counted against the right contract.

    How I worked

    • Sprint for planned, Kanban for incoming
    • Waiting is a status too
    • Traceable by default

    mosaicQ · Contract health

    Seeing a contract overrun before it happens

    Problem
    Hours, contracts and tickets lived in separate places. Overruns were discovered after the fact, when the only option left was to absorb the cost.
    Approach
    A panel on top of the Jira API that compares hours used with time elapsed, forecasts the date each contract runs out, and documents every threshold.
    Outcome
    Amendment conversations start while there is still room to negotiate, and slow-paced support contracts are flagged as a retention risk.

    How I worked

    • Every threshold written down
    • Warn early, not after
    • Pacing% used − % time elapsedabove +10 pp is accelerated, below −15 pp is slow
    • Runwayremaining hours ÷ avg. burn of last 3 monthsforecast date the cap is reached
    • SLAbusiness hours, Mon–Fri 8–178 / 24 / 48 / 72 h by priority

    mosaicQ · Project & portfolio

    A portfolio planned against real capacity

    Problem
    Every project had its own schedule, and nobody could answer the commercial question "can we take on another contract?" without guessing.
    Approach
    One portfolio view by methodology phase (Immersion to Deploy), with capacity measured from a 12-week delivery average and a 20% buffer kept free.
    Outcome
    Overrun warnings appear before the deadline, and sales can see the month when committed hours hit the team's ceiling.

    How I worked

    • Plan against real capacity
    • Keep a 20% buffer
    • Decisions cite indicators

    mosaicQ · Indicators

    One panel for the whole operation, built on the Jira API

    Problem
    The data was all in Jira, but spread across projects and boards. Answering "are we keeping up?" or "who is overloaded?" meant opening a dozen filters.
    Approach
    A panel written in Google Apps Script on top of the Jira API that joins development and support: throughput, lead time, velocity per project, sprint capacity with a buffer, workload per person and hours against contracts.
    Outcome
    Blocked work, overloaded people and contracts close to their limit show up before someone has to ask, and management meetings start from the same numbers.

    How I worked

    • Dev and support in one view
    • Buffer before commitment
    • Show blocked, not just busy

    mosaicQ · Risk & continuity

    Contingency plans decided before they are needed

    Problem
    The indicators showed danger signs, but nobody had decided in advance what to do when they lit up, including the risk of losing a client.
    Approach
    Each risk gets probability × impact, an early-warning signal tied to a real indicator, prevention actions and a contingency plan with a trigger and an owner.
    Outcome
    A monthly client health score and plans ready for the first 48 hours, instead of improvising under pressure.

    How I worked

    • Decide before it's urgent
    • Every plan has an owner
    • Blameless post-mortems
